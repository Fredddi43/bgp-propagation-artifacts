\section{Forecasting the reach of a leak}\label{sec:forecast}

Given the first seconds of an event, which ASes will be on its collector-visible paths two hours after onset?
\Cref{sec:prop} shows that leaks and invalid-origin changes show a large share of this set within 30\,s on average.
The events that keep spreading are the open problem, and we forecast leaks, the largest group of them.
The slow-regime experiments score the forecast set against persistence, the forecast that no AS joins after the issue time, the onset plus 30 or 60\,s.
R1 and R2 score the ranking of the ASes that join, where persistence has no ranking and history is the reference.

We use three setups (\Cref{tab:forecast-setups}).
The slow-regime experiments came first and select leaks by their final size, which is unknown at issue time, so they are exploratory.
R1 is descriptive, because its period was open while we developed the methods, and R2 is a prespecified replication of R1.
The slow regime also differs from R1 and R2 in training population and threshold calibration.

\subsection{Slow-regime experiments}\label{sec:forecast-legacy}

\paragraph{Task and candidates.}
The slow regime holds the leaks that still grow at the issue time and reach at least 200 ASes by two hours.
After the onset rule of \Cref{sec:prop}, 2025 has 693 training and 423 test events, and one training event has no 30-s input.
% round 7 (review-r6 W5, 1,115 / 1,116): 693 + 423 = 1,116 = the population of sections/09-discussion.tex; 692 training events at 30 s
%   (forecast-facts.json) because one has no 30-s input (09-discussion.tex src comment, lines 32--33 and 72--73).
Models rank candidate ASes in the two-hop neighbourhood of the seed ASes, the ASes observed by issue time, in the CAIDA relationship graph~\cite{luckie2013relationships}.
We cap this neighbourhood at \num{3000} ASes, and it holds 92\,\% of the target on average.
We score the full forecast set, seeds included, by Jaccard against $\set{2h}$, and report the gain over persistence.
Intervals are paired event-bootstrap 95\,\% intervals within one fit.
% src: paper/generated/forecast-facts.json (692 / 423; target coverage 0.919 at 30 s)

\paragraph{Methods.}
We compare a ladder of methods of increasing complexity.
The \emph{historical baseline} forecasts the final size from the $k$ nearest past onsets and ranks candidates by how often they joined completed training events.
Its \emph{online} variant also adds completed test events as they end.
The \emph{independent cascade}~\cite{kempe2003influence} spreads the route over the CAIDA graph with probabilities by relationship type.
Time-series foundation models forecast the AS count only: Chronos-2~\cite{ansari2025chronos2} zero-shot and with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}, Moirai~\cite{woo2024moirai} and TimesFM~\cite{das2023timesfm}.

The graph models are a GraphSAGE (SAGE)~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} with an aggregation per relationship type, a GAT~\cite{velickovic2018gat}, and the temporal TGN~\cite{rossi2020tgn} and DyRep~\cite{trivedi2019dyrep}.
They share node features: degree, customer cone, onset visibility, valley counts and participation in completed past events.
They share a membership head and a threshold tuned on training data.

\paragraph{History reaches most of the graph-model gain.}
At 30\,s, SAGE raises Jaccard from 0.509 to 0.708, a gain of $+0.198$ [$+0.178$, $+0.219$] (\Cref{fig:ladder}).
The historical baseline reaches 0.698, and GAT, TGN and DyRep-style reach 0.686--0.696, just below it.
The paired difference of SAGE over the historical baseline is only $+0.010$ [$+0.007$, $+0.013$], and over online history $+0.002$ [$-0.001$, $+0.005$].
When we train on 2023 and 2024 and test on 2025, online history exceeds SAGE by $+0.017$ at 30\,s and $+0.019$ at 60\,s.
If we resample UTC days instead of events, no interval end moves by more than 0.002.
The independent cascade falls below persistence, and the count-only foundation models match its count error (\Cref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{The gain lies in what is known at onset.}
The gain over persistence falls with the horizon: $+0.198$, $+0.121$, $+0.047$ and $+0.016$ at 30\,s, 1, 5 and 15\,min.
To test what the model knows at onset, we shift its trigger by $-30$\,s, so that it sees only the onset observations.
It still reaches Jaccard 0.574, against 0.045 for persistence.
Most of the Jaccard gain therefore comes from what the model knows at onset, not from the early spread of the event.
It knows the seed ASes, the actor and the topology, and training on large leaks only gives it their typical final size.
This finding shaped R1 and R2: they score only the ranking of new members, the ASes that join after issue time.

\subsection{Retrospective evaluation (R1)}\label{sec:forecast-r1}

\paragraph{Task and metric.}
R1 forecasts the selected leak candidates of 2025 (\Cref{tab:forecast-setups}).
Both rules use quantities known only after the event, and R1 has no size threshold.
Training runs from January to June 2025, validation to August, and the test split holds the \num{3543} events of September to December.
Events of one actor with consecutive onsets at most 300\,s apart form incidents, and we remove incidents that cross a split boundary.
The seed ASes are those observed by issue time, and the \emph{new members} are target ASes outside the seeds.

The primary metric is the average precision (AP) of the ranking of candidates over the new members, averaged over events.
Its denominator includes new members outside the candidate set, which therefore count as never retrieved.
% layout (round 5, page limit): "Tied scores form an unordered group, and AP is the exact expectation over random orders within each group"
%   left the body; sections/app-protocol.tex "Metrics" keeps the tie rule.
AP exists for the \num{2661} events with new members at 30\,s and the \num{2130} at 60\,s.
\Cref{app:protocol} defines the metrics and gives the configuration, loss, thresholds and history timing (\Cref{tab:recipe}).

\paragraph{Arms and tests.}
The core is a two-by-two design.
One factor is message passing over the AS graph, SAGE or none.
The other is the history feature, on or off.
All four arms share candidates, features, loss, configuration and selection rule, and each has five training seeds.

Online history, the online variant of the historical baseline, ranks candidates by their participation in completed past events.
It uses the shared candidates or its native ones, every AS with such participation, and we call the second variant native history.
% round 6 (panel-r6 S9 / T6, panel-r5 NEW-14): gloss of "native history" (tab:r1 row, sections/09-discussion.tex, "Secondary metrics").
Ordinary intervals resample training seeds and then UTC-day blocks (121 days), and Holm's procedure~\cite{holm1979} corrects each family.
R1 counts its 90 failed cells, all of TGN arms, as failures (\Cref{app:protocol}).

\subsection{Prespecified replication (R2)}\label{sec:forecast-newperiod}

R2 repeats the R1 design on a later period, after we had fitted every model.
We fixed its arms, tests and outcome wording before the evaluator joined labels to forecasts (\Cref{tab:chronology}).

\paragraph{Data.}
We split by observed onset in UTC.
Training covers 1 January 2025 to 1 April 2026, calibration 1 April to 1 September 2026, and evaluation 1 September to 16 October 2026.
The cohort uses the rules of R1 and holds \num{1311} identified events.
The \num{1289} events with their observed onset inside the window form the test set, and none of them is censored.
An earlier baseline run used a May 2026 split, so R2 is no pristine holdout.
% src: docs/paper/forecasting-improvement-20261017/MANUSCRIPT-DRAFT-METHODS.md; REVIEW-PACKET-NEWPERIOD-INPUTS.md section 1; primary.json censoring (1,289 reference, 0 censored)

\paragraph{Arms and hypotheses.}
R2 keeps the two-by-two core of R1 at both horizons, with five training seeds, and adds the frozen SAGE model of R1 with history.
Six contrasts per horizon form the primary Holm family of twelve at family-wise level 0.05.
C1 to C4 are those of R1, C5 compares the frozen model with online history, and C6 with the refitted model.
A separate family compares both SAGE models with online history on its native candidates.
One seed of the frozen model fails at 30\,s, so C5 and C6 at 30\,s enter the family with $p=1$ (\Cref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Outcome wording.}
We named C2 at 30\,s the headline contrast before the label join.
We call a contrast \emph{resolved} if the half-width of its 95\,\% interval is at most 0.01 AP, a planning target for precision.
A Holm rejection means that one arm ranks new members better.
\Cref{tab:r2} calls a resolved non-rejection \emph{bounded}, which means only that its interval is narrow, not that the arms are equivalent.

\paragraph{Protected labels.}
A custodian computed the R2 labels, and a freeze and a seal fixed every model, threshold, input and forecast by hash.
The evaluator then joined the labels once for the primary result, and the secondary lanes needed two further disclosed reads (\Cref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Limits of the record.}
The controls are procedural, and we operated them ourselves.
The custodian shares the user account of the project, so it is an audited software boundary, not enforced secrecy.
No third party timestamped the freeze or the seal, and anyone can recompute the labels from public archives.
The label file existed before we committed the outcome wording, and four failed runs that built it read label records.
Nobody inspected an outcome, but no external record shows this.
From 27 October, one AI session approved each step and took scientific decisions together with the authors (\Cref{sec:genai}).

\paragraph{Primary results.}
AP exists for the \num{1004} of \num{1289} test events with new members at 30\,s and the 842 at 60\,s.
Intervals resample training seeds and 45 UTC-day blocks.
They are ordinary 95\,\% intervals, not simultaneous and not Holm-adjusted, so an interval can exclude zero without a Holm rejection (C6).

For the common AP contrasts, R2 repeats every Holm rejection and every non-rejection of R1, but C2 at 60\,s changes sign (\Cref{tab:r2}, \Cref{fig:r1r2}).
Estimates agree less: R2 values of C3 at both horizons and of C2 at 60\,s lie outside the R1 intervals.

The headline contrast, C2 at 30\,s, is resolved without a rejection, with $+0.0003$ [$-0.0075$, $+0.0079$].
We therefore detect no AP difference between SAGE with history and online history on the same candidates, which does not show equivalence.
At 60\,s, C2 is also resolved without a rejection.

Day blocks do not capture a leaker that recurs on several days.
A revision analysis also resamples leakers, and no interval of C2 or of the native contrast then changes whether it includes zero (\Cref{tab:actor-bootstrap}).
Under the conservative two-way draw of leakers and days, C2 in R2 has half-widths of 0.0105 and 0.0108, above the planned 0.01.
