\section{Background and related work}\label{sec:back}

\paragraph{Origin validation.}
RPKI lets prefix holders authorize an AS to originate a prefix through a ROA~\cite{rfc6480}.
Route origin validation compares an announcement with these ROAs~\cite{rfc6811}.
An announcement is \emph{valid} when a covering ROA permits both its origin and its prefix length.
It is \emph{not found} when no ROA covers the prefix, and \emph{invalid} when covering ROAs exist but none permits it.
We call the not-found state \emph{unknown}.
Configuration errors can make announcements invalid~\cite{chung2019rpki,li2026demystifying}, so invalidity alone does not show a hijack.

\paragraph{ROV and observed reach.}
ROAs limit propagation only where networks use them to reject invalid routes~\cite{gilad2017}.
Passive data make ROV deployment hard to infer, and controlled announcements separate origin validation from other route preferences~\cite{reuter2018rov}.
Testart \etal count the collector peers that carry invalid announcements~\cite{testart2020filter}, and the Kentik reports measure how far invalid routes propagate~\cite{madory2022kentik,madory2024kentik}.
RoVista measures ROV per AS with data-plane probes~\cite{li2023rovista}, and RScope tracks the visibility of invalid routes over years~\cite{li2026rscope}.
These studies measure reach, not how a single event spreads after its first observation.
We measure this time course for all three RPKI states and for the return (\Cref{sec:prop,sec:recovery}), and public RPKI beacons repeat their static result (\Cref{sec:beacons}).

\paragraph{Convergence and recovery.}
After a route change, BGP can explore other paths before it converges~\cite{labovitz2000delayed}, and BGP beacons make this process observable~\cite{mao2003convergence}.
Route flap damping can suppress a route that is withdrawn and announced again~\cite{mao2002damping}, and some withdrawn routes stay as zombies~\cite{fontugne2019zombies}.
ARTEMIS measures recovery in controlled hijacks with active mitigation, from the arrival of the hijacked route until the legitimate route returns~\cite{sermpezis2018artemis}.
Our pairs of archive transitions each start at their own first observation (\Cref{sec:recovery}), so our times do not compare directly with these delays.

\paragraph{Collector visibility.}
Collectors see only the routes that their peers export, and a hijacker can keep its route away from these peers~\cite{milolidakis2023}.
Collector placement and redundant peers shape what an analysis can see~\cite{zhang2007,alfroy2024mvp}, and correlated peers can bias estimates of the affected share of ASes~\cite{sermpezis2021impact}.
Our target is therefore the AS set on observed paths, and we check results per collector.
RIPE NCC announces beacon prefixes with valid, invalid and unknown states~\cite{ripe2011beacons}, which we compare at the same collectors (\Cref{sec:beacons}).
A missing invalid route does not show that a peer applies ROV, because an upstream AS or a route server may have rejected it~\cite{reuter2018rov,hlavacek2023rov}.

\paragraph{Route leaks.}
A route leak propagates a route beyond its intended scope~\cite{rfc7908}.
Detectors compare paths with inferred customer, provider and peer relationships~\cite{gao2001relationships,cloudflare2022leakdetection}, so errors in these relationships cause false detections.
Inference methods such as ProbLink and TopoScope reduce these errors~\cite{jin2019problink,jin2020toposcope}, and RFC~9234 adds roles to BGP sessions to prevent leaks~\cite{rfc9234}.
Our leak identifier inherits the uncertainty of the relationships it uses (\Cref{sec:data}).

\paragraph{Detection, early forecasts and baselines.}
Argus detects hijacks by joining control-plane anomalies with data-plane reachability~\cite{shi2012argus}, and DFOH detects hijacks with a forged origin~\cite{holterbach2024dfoh}.
Such detectors decide whether an event is an attack, while we measure and forecast the AS set that an event reaches over time.

Earlier work estimates the impact of a hijack from observations, or simulates propagation under assumed routing policies~\cite{sermpezis2021impact,furuness2023}.
SWIFT infers the extent of a remote failure from the first messages of a withdrawal burst~\cite{holterbach2017swift}.
We forecast the AS set of an event at two hours from its first seconds.
On temporal graphs, memory baselines such as EdgeBank are hard to beat~\cite{poursafaei2022,huang2023tgb}.
These tasks differ from ours, but they motivate our comparison with persistence and with a history of past events (\Cref{sec:forecast}).
Some networks also hijack again and again~\cite{testart2019serialhijackers}, so past events can carry information about future ones.
