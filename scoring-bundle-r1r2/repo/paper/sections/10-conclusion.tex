\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We replayed ten public collectors from January 2022 to August 2026 and followed origin changes and route leaks for two hours.
From the first observation of the new origin, invalid-origin events show the largest share of their final AS set within 30\,s.
This gap is sensitive to the event definition, because valid and unknown origins were often visible long before the identified change.
For events first observed near the change, the class differences in 2023--2026 are smaller, but this rule keeps far fewer valid than invalid events.
In 2023--2025, catalogue selection also raises the \floor of invalid events.

For gaps of at least 15 minutes between the identifiers, the return shows more of its final set early.
Day-block intervals exclude zero in three of five years.
Most matched pairs are flips of less than a minute, in which the observed return set is larger, for reasons that we did not test.
The relative reach of a controlled invalid beacon fell by about half in early 2024, and two upstream changes coincide with its April steps.

For forecasts of the ASes that join a leak, a history of past events is the reference to beat.
In exploratory tests on large leaks selected by final size, graph models gain up to $+0.20$ Jaccard over persistence, and history recovers most of it.
The retrospective evaluation R1 has no size threshold, but it also differs in training and calibration.
There, the set forecast of SAGE with history equals persistence for 95\,\% of events at 30\,s and 99.6\,\% at 60\,s.
In a prespecified replication on autumn 2026 (R2), at most 94\,\% and 97\,\% of these set forecasts add nothing.

In R2, six prespecified rejections favour online history in AP or precision at ten, and six favour SAGE in Jaccard against history's size rule.
Online history with its own, nearly complete candidate set reaches 0.02--0.03 more AP, in a comparison of complete systems.
In a post hoc R1 analysis on reconstructed inputs, online history gains 0.073 [0.059, 0.088] AP with the same-leaker-first ranking.
We did not test this ranking in R2.

The clock decides the class lead: the ordering holds at every collector but shrinks under a near-onset rule.
SAGE gains on leaks selected by final size, but on leaks selected at issue time its sets match persistence.
With seven of ten collectors, the R2 forecasts lose 0.07 to 0.12 Jaccard against the fixed target.
